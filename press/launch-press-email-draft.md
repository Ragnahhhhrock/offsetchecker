**Subject:** The follow-up to your offset account reporting: an independent check, to the cent

**Preheader:** ASIC found $55 million in offset failures. Offsetcheck verifies the maths line by line.

---

Hi [first name],

You covered ASIC's finding that banks paid more than `$55,000,000` in compensation over two years for offset account failures — accounts never opened, never linked, or quietly de-linked when a loan changed. In 77 per cent of reported cases, the bank had not detected the failure itself.

That reporting raised a question your readers are now asking: how do I know whether *my* offset is working — and if the bank offers me compensation, how do I know the figure is right?

Today we are launching **Offsetcheck**, an independent verification service that answers both questions. A customer uploads their loan and offset statements, and Offsetcheck re-calculates the interest charged and the offset benefit credited — line by line, to the cent — and lays the result next to the bank's figure.

Three points that may be relevant to your coverage:

1. **The gap ASIC could not assess.** ASIC's review explicitly did not examine how banks calculate interest. Offsetcheck checks exactly that: the daily interest arithmetic itself, not just whether an account was linked.
2. **Compensation can be checked, not just accepted.** Borrowers in your reporting — including a Brisbane couple offered more than `$5,000` — said they had no way to verify the bank's calculation. Offsetcheck produces the independent figure and the working behind it.
3. **The scale is growing.** Australians held `$349.1 billion` in offset accounts at March 2026, up 28 per cent in two years, across 55 per cent of home loan facilities.

Offsetcheck has no bank partnerships and no commissions. The answer is the same whoever asks.

We would be glad to offer you a demonstration, a walkthrough of the calculation method, or verified case files showing the working line by line. Embargoed material is available on request.
